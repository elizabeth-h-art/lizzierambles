> This is a fragile world. Hurtling through existence in orbit of the gas giant Inmana, Ithkar Ithkar harbours a fragment of human culture assailed on all sides from the Underworld, the innumerable planes of the Cosmic Well, and the four other moons that circulate in Inmana's orbit. But despite all of this, the cultures that rise and fall on Ithkar's surface continue to persist out of nothing less than spite, bargaining with the forces that would conspire to erase them. This is a mostly lawful world, in a sea of chaos and ambiguity.
> In the small corner of Ithkar known as the Arythian Basin, the Amict-Kocir, Okhurvan, and Arythian empires have all come and gone. Now, like the noble phoenix, the Adgrath Republic rises from the ashes of the Adgrath Empire that preceded it. A delicate balance brought on by conflict and compromise, it, too, is assailed on all sides; by a disgruntled emperor, by monarchist and radical factions within, by the Church, and by the merchants, whose ideological victory in the war has failed to satiate their greed. The Nyreans, oppressed for many years under the hand of the Empire, have done well in their end of the bargain, though many do not trust the fragile peace.
> Like a spinning-top that is shortly to fall, any number of failures could bring down the young Republic. These political squabbles are nothing new to this corner of Ithkar. Though, as it always has done, chaos and disorder provides the perfect backdrop for Magic. Magic-users have unprecedented ability to shape the world around them, though the magic they wield in turn shapes them. It will force them to liberate themselves from their chains, to resist order and hierarchy in whichever way the Magic seems fit. It will make wild animals of them all...

Welcome to Ithkar! This is my most long-standing and my most extensive worldbuilding project, with some elements dating back to 2021, although the current state of the project took form about a year ago. This is a world about magic, religion and identity, law, chaos and non-conformity, and about class conflict, democracy and authoritarianism.

My influences insofar as fantasy worldbuilding goes are pretty varied. Ursula K. Le Guin, my favourite writer of fantasy and science fiction, is definitely one of them. The anthropological approach that her science fiction takes, such as exploring gender in The Left Hand of Darkness and anarchism and authoritarianism in The Dispossessed, have been important here, as well as her views on fantasy worldbuilding and the way that magic works in her Earthsea series. 

To add a completely contradictory ingredient to the mix, the fiction of H.P. Lovecraft has also been a big influence here. Although in his time he was known as an enormous bigot, I think the ideas expressed in his fiction (of alienation, isolation, and things impossible to comprehend) have the potential to appeal to a lot of marginalised people (as I may discuss in a future article!), and so I hope to write a kind of eldritch horror in this setting that strikes out against oppression, rather than enthusiastically standing for it. The horrors in Ithkar are embedded into human institutions — states and religious organisations — which hurt the marginalised.

Other influences upon Ithkar include of course, J.R.R. Tolkien, as well as a smattering of modern fantasy writers such as Brandon Sanderson, and the films of Studio Ghibli. The Dark Souls series and it's related games in the soulslike genre are also an influence, as well as OSR-style roleplaying games. I love the sense of space that can be created in these kinds of games, whether through a video game level or a hex crawl, and the sense of cosmic insignificance in the face of a universe where death comes quick.

Now onto the worldbuilding!
# Cosmology

Ithkar is a moon, slightly smaller than the Earth (21,500 miles in circumference to Earth's 24,900), which orbits around the enormous, green-blue gas giant Inmana along with four other spherical moons on the Material Plane. Each of these moons operates on laws of magic foreign to those of Ithkar, is exceedingly difficult to reach, and populated by bizarre and fantastical creatures outside of the realm of possibility on Ithkar. 

Aside from the Material Plane upon which Ithkar exists, there are the Immaterial Planes. There are many debates among scholars on Ithkar regarding the nature of the Immaterial Planes, how best to represent them, and the structure of the overall system. What cannot be debated, however, is that the planes appear in some manner of a line — with the world of Ithkar on one end, and the base elements of reality on another. The closest Immaterial Plane to the material, and the one with the most obvious effects on it, is the Otherworld. As one proceeds further and further away from the world of Ithkar, elements of reality start to "flake off" and fragment, rending the laws of physics mutable and malleable as reality begins to break apart, culminating eventually in the "Elementary Forms." This enables the creation of demiplanes by great magical manipulators, which become more common and easier to create the farther from the Material Plane one strays. The sequence of planes has been given many names, the "Cosmic Well," the "Cosmic River," and the "Cosmological Field Series" being among them.

Some beings are noted to hold a presence which spans multiple of the Immaterial Planes. Indeed, the boundaries between the planes begin to blur as one progresses toward the Elementary Forms, rending it impossible to say how many planes there truly are.
# Magic

Magic upon Ithkar comes about through the channelling of the extraplanar spirits, known as Otherworlders, which affect all things on the Material Plane. Otherworlders come in many varieties, including Phantoms, Spirits, and Fiends, and Spirits, also known as Khazarin, are a diverse class in and of themselves. Magical aptitude relies upon convincing these spirits, whether through force, deception, charm or bargaining, to give their aid, and the diverse Pathways which lead to magical ability rely on any of these methods or a combination thereof as applied to any given Otherworlder creature.

The Druidic Circle of the Nyreans, for example, comprise an ancient and sacrosanct order with their own distinct magical Pathway. Magical ability here relies on the Druids' maintenance of the sacred natural spaces in the Valley of Nyrea, from which the Khazarin of the Valley derive their power. In exchange, the Khazarin offer out this power in select doses, which empowers the Nyreans and safeguards them from harm, such as by avoiding famine-inducing drought or eluding the Adgrathi forces which oppress them.

Otherworlders, especially those who deal out their magic on a more tit-for-tat basis, are notoriously fickle and chaotic, and sometimes resist hierarchical organisation. This is an effect that rubs off on magic-users, with some often ending up with a more chaotic bent to their personality, or even physical changes which make them resemble the Otherworlders that they manipulate. This chaos-inducing effect has been known to subvert magical organisations from within, such as the Adgrathi Guild of the Ancient Arts, which progressed from a pro-Imperial organisation to an organisation which ended up subverting the Empire from within. 
# Human Diversity

Given that the Otherworlders can influence the likeness of magic-users with whom they become intwined, it also then follows that human groups who operate in areas where certain Otherworlder influences are strong become gradually altered by the Khazarin which surround them, and either favour them or do not. Owing to the chaotic nature of the Khazarin, these changes are sometimes merely cosmetic, or sometimes very affecting, and appear to be somewhat random. Below are a few such people groups who are influenced in certain ways by their Khazarin environments.

The **Nyreans** are the native people of the Valley of Nyrea, now part of the Adgrathi Republic. The Nyreans have relatively darker skin compared to their neighbours, and generally live longer lives — up to 120 years. They also have slightly pointed ears, keen senses, and are built small and slim.
The **Tharroski** moved into the land which is now Adgrath prior to the arrival of the Arythians, and are a subgroup of Orlovi. They are grey-skinned, sturdily built, and their skin has a unique texture — patches of typical human skin interweave with reptilian scales.
**Arythians** have olive skin and an average build. They have few unique physical characteristics, although as with all human groups, display a unique and distinct culture. They have historically dominated the area that has come to be known as the Arythian Basin. The Adgrathi people are a subset of this group which speak the Adgrathi language and inhabit the north of the Arythian Basin.
# Conflict & Strife

![Map of the Adgrath Republic and surroundings](/assets/Adgrath_Republic_and_Surroundings.jpeg)

Conflict between humans takes all forms in Ithkar, and takes place for ideological, religious, economic, and other reasons. In the Adgrath Republic, the area which this project focuses on, tensions run high between a milleu of competing factions. The Republic was recently proclaimed following the Adgrathi Civil War, which saw the expulsion of the ruling House Castor and the proclamation of republicanism, although House Castor are conniving to return to their position. The war ended with an uneasy peace between republican factions, financed by the wealthy Syndicate of Adgrathi Merchants, and the remaining loyalist noble houses — the most notable being House Kaervos and House Edarc. 

As a result, the new Republic consists of three provinces under democratic control, who elect their own representatives to the Council of Aedril and the Republic Assembly, and two remaining feudal-aristocratic provinces. This peace cannot last — conservative factions, including the noble houses, the prevailing Church of the Sea Mother, and the extant members of House Castor, wish to restore monarchy, whereas the Syndicate want to consolidate control, and republican extremist factions, such as the Nyrean Resistance, desire a more total move towards democracy. The nature of Adgrathi democracy is questionable — many Assembly members, even in supposedly republican provinces, are appointed rather than elected, or elections are rigged, or corruption influences Assembly Members' voting choices. Soon, the inherently unstable Republic will either buckle under the pressure, or transform into something entirely different.
# Conclusion

Hopefully, you enjoyed this brief summary of my worldbuilding project thus far. This project is expansive, and I intend to expand it ever more, but the most important part of the project to my mind is internal consistency and thematic coherence. I hope that the worldbuilders reading this (you know who you are!) can be inspired by it and pull a few ideas to make their own projects more colourful. After all, learning from each other is the most important thing we can do.
